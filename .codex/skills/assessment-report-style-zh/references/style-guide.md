# Style Guide

This file distills the stable writing moves shared across the local reports in `/Users/lulu/AIWork/大佬的测评/前人测评报告`.

## What the reports consistently do

### 1. Start from a recognizable result label

The reports usually begin by naming the score, type, or dominant pattern in plain language.

Examples of the pattern:

- “你在【伙伴模式】维度的得分为：高水平”
- “你的金钱性格是：……”
- “夜间型且睡程短的你，属于‘蜂鸟系’”

Rule:

- Name the result early
- Do not hide the answer behind suspense

### 2. Move from description to mechanism

The reports rarely stop at “你是怎样的人”. They move one level deeper:

- what this pattern looks like in life
- why it may have formed
- what belief, need, or interaction logic may sit underneath

Rule:

- Every strong section should contain both observable pattern and underlying mechanism

### 3. Alternate warmth and explanation

The voice is not cold. It often sounds like:

- understanding
- gently interpretive
- emotionally literate

But the warmth is paired with analysis rather than slogans.

Rule:

- Validate experience, then explain it
- Do not over-comfort without saying anything concrete

### 4. Translate trait language into lived scenes

The reports repeatedly turn abstractions into daily situations:

- homework friction
- being scolded
- staying up late
- spending money for safety
- withdrawing in relationships

Rule:

- Convert each abstract dimension into scenes the user can picture

### 5. Explain impact in relational or developmental terms

The reports often answer:

- what the child may learn from this
- what the person may repeat in relationships
- what long-term loop may be strengthened

Rule:

- The consequence should feel plausible and cumulative
- Avoid catastrophic prediction

### 6. End with one or two practical moves

Advice in the source reports usually:

- starts small
- is behaviorally specific
- can be practiced this week

Rule:

- Prefer one small repeatable move over a complete self-reinvention plan

## Common section patterns

### Pattern A: Dimension feedback

Common blocks:

- current level statement
- why this matters
- current state
- possible causes or impacts
- growth suggestion

### Pattern B: Archetype / type report

Common blocks:

- type name and tagline
- overt style or habit pattern
- deeper belief
- hidden pursuit or unmet need
- risk or cost

### Pattern C: Long-form narrative

Common blocks:

- what the original family / environment felt like
- how it shaped self
- how it shaped relationships
- what inner vow, need, or coping style formed
- what kind of family the person may create next

## Sentence-level characteristics

### Good moves

- “这可能是因为……”
- “长期如此，可能会……”
- “你可能会发现……”
- “更深一层看……”
- “需要留意的是……”
- “可以先从……开始”

### Avoid

- pure slogan sentences
- exaggerated certainty
- excessive rhetorical questions
- long chains of theory without lived meaning
- copying the same emotional cadence every paragraph

## Tone boundaries

### Allowed

- warm
- reflective
- psychologically informed
- gently interpretive

### Not allowed

- diagnostic certainty
- guru voice
- shame-heavy language
- fake authority
- empty healing language

## Safety guardrails

### Do not diagnose

Do not turn score feedback into medical or psychiatric diagnosis.

Use:

- “可能”
- “常见的是”
- “这类模式下，容易……”

Avoid:

- “你就是……症”
- “这说明你患有……”

### Do not invent evidence

If using theory, keep it general unless the user gave an explicit source. Do not fabricate named studies or experts.

### Do not remove agency

Even low-score feedback should leave space for change.

Good framing:

- “这是一种可以被觉察和调整的模式”
- “先从一个小动作开始”

Bad framing:

- “你的人格已经定型”
- “你注定会……”

### Do not weaponize parent-facing content

For parenting reports, do not write as if the parent is the enemy. Name harm clearly, but preserve dignity and room for repair.

## Compression rule

If the user asks for a shorter report, compress by:

1. reducing repeated explanation
2. keeping one mechanism instead of three
3. keeping one suggestion instead of two

Do not compress by deleting:

- the result label
- the core mechanism
- the likely impact
